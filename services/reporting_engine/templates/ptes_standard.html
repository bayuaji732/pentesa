5. Testing Methodology & Tooling Disclosure

The engagement combined automated deterministic security tools with specialized AI agents operating under strict zero-shell sandboxes:

  • OWASP WSTG v4.2 Framework: Systematic evaluation across 12 vulnerability categories.
  • Deterministic Tooling: Nuclei v3 (vulnerability templates), Ffuf (controlled fuzzing), and Python HTTP analysis engine (crawling and probing).
  • AI Specialized Agents: Planner, Recon, WSTG, Authorization, Validation, and Reporting agents governed by the Pentesa WorkflowSupervisor.
6. Rules of Engagement (RoE)

All testing conformed to pre-authorized operational guardrails:

  • Rate Limiting: Maximum outbound requests capped to avoid denial of service.
  • Testing Window: Authorized from {{ ctx.start_date }} through {{ ctx.end_date }}.
  • Destructive Testing Policy: Destructive commands, data exfiltration, and service disruptions were strictly disallowed.

13. Business Impact Analysis

Identified vulnerabilities present direct exposure across key operational dimensions:

  • Data Confidentiality & Privacy: Risk of unauthorized access to sensitive records and user identity data.
  • Regulatory Compliance: Exposure to potential non-compliance under GDPR, HIPAA, and PCI-DSS requirements for access control and injection handling.
  • System Integrity: Potential for unauthorized state modification on vulnerable API endpoints.

16. Assessment Limitations & Caveats

The penetration test represents a point-in-time security audit conducted under authorized constraints:

  • Testing was confined exclusively to the designated target hosts and endpoints.
  • Third-party authentication providers and external cloud services were not subject to denial-of-service or infrastructure attacks.
  • New vulnerabilities introduced via subsequent code deployments require re-testing.
